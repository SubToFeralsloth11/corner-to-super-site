Corner to Super

A first-person supermarket game: grow a 12 × 8 m corner shop into a 28 × 24 m supermarket. Your stock arrives by ice-cream van, which fires the boxes out of its roof speaker.

Order on the store PC, catch the boxes, stock the shelves, set prices, scan groceries, count Australian change by hand, pay the rent and the loans, knock walls through and hire staff. It's modelled on Supermarket Simulator and built on the asset pipeline of an earlier game, Mimic Mart.

This page is a log of how the game was built. The game's repository is private. None of the pictures here are in-game screenshots; they're renders from the asset pipeline.

What's in the game

Everything below is built and covered by headless tests. None of it has been checked in a window yet; see status.

📦 Stocking

  • The ShopOS PC on the counter, with a Market app: cart, cost arrows (▲▼) after the overnight price drift, "On shelves N · In boxes M" for each product, and Restock to full.
  • Carry stock boxes, open them, and hold E to put items on a slot one at a time. R takes them back. Fold the empty box and put it in the bin.
  • Slot capacity depends on the pack shape (6 cans, 3 bags, 12 stacked noodles…). Slots glow green or red, and a red slot says why ("Needs a drinks cooler").
  • Live shelf price labels. Press F on a label to reprice it, and every label for that product updates.
  • Shelf stock is drawn with MultiMesh. Rear rows are hidden beyond 25 m.

🍦 The delivery van

  • 3–6 s after you order, the ice-cream van skids into the car park. It swings its roof speaker round, fires the boxes into the yellow bay along ballistic arcs, honks, and leaves with a burnout U-turn.
  • Bay spots are reserved as each box flies and filled from the van's side. A second layer and an overflow grid take big orders. Lost boxes come back to the bay.
  • The van carries 30 boxes a trip; a bigger order comes in several trips for one $5 fee. Furniture arrives as flat-packs, and the van rocks when it fires one.
  • It brakes, honks and waits if you stand in its lane.
  • Press E with empty hands to catch a box mid-air (+5 XP). From day 3, now and then a box goes wild, bounces off a parked car and sets off its alarm.

🛒 Customers & checkout

  • 14 shopper models, each with a shopping list, a basket or a trolley, and speech bubbles. They find the nearest stocked slot, reach in, and buy or refuse depending on your price.
  • Empty shelves and high prices are recorded as lost sales. A customer who has waited 90 s at the till drops the basket and leaves.
  • At the till: customers unload, you click each item to scan it, and the POS screen updates. Card customers need the exact amount typed (a wrong amount is DECLINED). Cash customers need their change counted by hand from AUD-style notes and coins.
  • Australian cash rounding: cash totals round to the nearest 5c and card payments are exact. Give too little and they say "I'm still owed…"; give too much and you lose the difference. An optional Change helper highlights the right coins.
  • Smokes are sold over the counter from the cigarette wall behind the till.

🧾 Days, bills, loans, saves

  • PREP at 07:30 with the clock paused. Flip the door sign to OPEN; at 22:00 no new customers come in; hold E on the sign to end the day. 08:00–22:00 lasts 12 real minutes (8, 12, 16 or 20 in Options).
  • The end-of-day receipt bills rent, electricity, wages and loan instalments. Unpaid bills get a 10% late fee. After three unpaid nights in a row, the bank repossesses the store.
  • Bank app for loans (a $1,000 loan costs $108 a night for 10 nights) and early repayment.
  • 3 save slots and an autosave every night. The HUD shows cash, profit and stars; hold Tab for the clipboard.

⭐ Progression

  • 16 levels. XP comes from customers, items and perfect change. A LEVEL N banner with confetti lists what unlocked.
  • 9 licences (Starter, Dairy, Frozen, Produce, Pantry, Household, Bottle shop, Deli, Tobacco) open up the 48 products.
  • Market prices drift ±4% a day. The Pricing app has bulk actions and the Stats app has a 14-day chart.
  • Reputation stars, and cleanliness: spills, litter and dropped baskets. Mop them up before customers start complaining.

🧱 Build mode & expansions

  • The Furniture app orders flat-packs. In build mode (B), place on a 0.25 m grid and rotate with Q or the scroll wheel; a red ghost says why it can't go there. Empty fixtures sell for 50%.
  • The Expansions app shows a plot map. Expansions E1–E8 grow the store over 42 cells, and the old walls break into falling pieces with dust, a rumble and camera shake.
  • The stockroom shed (S1–S3) has a roller door and storage racks.
  • The navmesh rebakes on a worker thread after changes, and walkers re-route.

🧑‍💼 Staff

  • Cashiers, restockers and cleaners, each with three tiers, a hire fee and a nightly wage, managed in the Staff app.
  • They walk in from the footpath at PREP and go home at night.
  • Cashiers scan and count exact change. Restockers refill shelves from rack boxes only. Cleaners mop messes and bin boxes.
  • Customers prefer staffed tills.

🥫 Content

  • 48 products, all orderable, stockable, buyable and sellable. Apples, oranges, tomatoes, potatoes and wine alternate between two meshes, and loose produce varies slightly in colour.
  • 18 fixture types, including a cooler, a dairy multideck, freezers, a produce stand, and a deli corner with a slicer.
  • Ceiling aisle signs appear once three categories are on the floor. Outside: verge trees and parked cars that come and go with trade.
  • Ambience (traffic, birds, room tone, crowd murmur) and six music tracks with crossfades.

🎛️ Menus & tutorial

  • The main menu sits over a slow dolly shot of a stocked store. It has Continue, New game, Load, Options, Controls, Credits and a Sandbox picker (start level 1–16, money $600–$1,000,000, no bills).
  • Options: sensitivity, FOV, Low/Medium/High graphics, six volume buses, the change helper and day length.
  • An 8-step day-1 tutorial, with a gold arrow to the bay. Dale walks up, hands over the keys and walks off.
  • Scan flash, a cash counter that rolls up, and an F3 performance overlay.

📰 Review additions

  • Catch the box and van misfires that set off a car alarm.
  • Store phone (level 6, $500): press P anywhere to open the Market and Furniture apps.
  • My Store look: floor vinyl, sign, shelf trim and uniform colours, with one new option at each level from 2 to 16.
  • Sunday's Corner Courier review quotes real customer speech bubbles from the week. MegaMart sale weeks make customers refuse high markups twice as often.

    How it was built

    One person (the user) directed a main agent, which planned the work and handed milestones to coding agents. Nobody played the game by hand: bots played it headless and tests checked the results.

    The rules it ran by

    • Plan first. Ten docs (vision, GDD, economy, the van, architecture, reuse, assets, visuals, milestones with acceptance checks) were written before any code.
    • Grilled before starting. Seven questions settled how to run it: straight through M0 to M9, bots and playtest checklists instead of manual play, tune to the pacing table, a Sandbox picker, Kevin MacLeod CC-BY music, clean the disk as you go, then a critique-and-fix loop.
    • Nothing on screen. All checks were headless. Anything that needs eyes (screenshots, real fps) was written down as a deferred check.
    • Atomic commits, pushed after each change. Agents stage only their own files.

    gt: the test runner that unblocked it

    Early on, Godot runs on an 8 GB Mac were risky, so testing was put on hold and M1 and M2 were written blind. The user then wrote gt, a headless Godot test runner. It queues every agent's run behind one shared lock, waits for free memory, caches results for unchanged files, and lets agents waiting on the same run share one result.

    Its first run on the blind M1 code gave 136 passed, 2 failed and about 300 errors. The suite had grown to 1225 passed, 0 failed twelve hours after the first commit.

    Bots instead of hands

    Acceptance items that need someone to play are checked by scripted players that use the real controls. StockBot clicks the PC's buttons, carries boxes and fills shelves. TillBot sits at the till, scans, types card amounts and counts change. Tours play whole days (a 3-day day tour, a progression tour and a staff day with 79 customers). M9's soak bot plays 30 days in about 4 minutes and logs pacing, errors and stuck NPCs.

    When things went wrong

    • Mouse look dead, POS screen flickering (user report, 18:08): the crosshair control was eating mouse input, and the screen was z-fighting with its glass. Both fixed by 18:21.
    • Broken main (19:34): one agent's git commit -a swept another agent's half-done edits into main. It was fixed within minutes.
    • Review round 1: one crash (selling a till with a queue), 6 major and 13 minor issues. The fixes came with regression tests; four of them (#1, #2, #3, #17) were shown failing on the old code first.
